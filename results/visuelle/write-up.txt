1. What Was Done

The existing forecasting experiments were extended to investigate whether Google Trends search interest improves weekly sales forecasting beyond retail history, calendar variables, and planned discounts. Weekly sales series were constructed for categories, colours, and fabrics, with a stock-availability filter based on dated restock records. Forecasts cover four- and eight-week horizons.

The comparison includes last-value and trailing-four-week-average baselines, Ridge regression, XGBoost, and LightGBM. Both tree-based models were evaluated with and without lagged search-interest features. All models used identical rows, calendar-date splits, and forecast horizons. Paired bootstrap confidence intervals over test weeks were used to evaluate the effect of adding search features.

2. What Was Found

Both gradient-boosted tree models substantially outperformed the last-value baseline. On the test set, XGBoost reduced MAE by approximately 36% at four weeks and 47% at eight weeks, while LightGBM reduced MAE by approximately 35% and 47%, respectively. XGBoost achieved the lowest test WAPE without search at four weeks (18.97%), while LightGBM achieved the lowest at eight weeks (21.39%). The performance difference between the two models was relatively small at the eight-week horizon.

Adding search features did not produce a consistent improvement. For LightGBM, search changed test WAPE by +0.22 percentage points at four weeks (95% CI: −0.33 to +0.74) and −0.54 percentage points at eight weeks (95% CI: −1.41 to +0.14). Both confidence intervals include zero, indicating no statistically reliable change.

For XGBoost, adding search increased WAPE by +1.05 percentage points at four weeks (95% CI: +0.39 to +1.88) and +1.14 percentage points at eight weeks (95% CI: −0.13 to +2.70). The four-week result indicates a statistically reliable deterioration, while the eight-week result does not establish a reliable change.

Overall, the results support using the no-search boosted-tree models as the primary benchmarks: XGBoost for the four-week horizon and LightGBM for the eight-week horizon, based on test WAPE. Incomplete search coverage and imperfect stock reconstruction remain limitations. The data was split by calendar target week: training before 26 November 2018, validation from 26 November 2018 through 5 August 2019, and testing from 12 August 2019 onward. Further work should focus on improving stock reconstruction and search-data coverage and alignment.

3. What Changed from the Proposal, and Why?

Compared with the original proposal, the analysis was extended to include calendar-time weekly forecasting, stock-availability filtering, four- and eight-week forecast horizons, and direct comparisons of boosted-tree models with and without lagged Google Trends features. Paired bootstrap confidence intervals over test weeks were also introduced to assess whether search features produced reliable changes in WAPE.

These extensions were introduced to investigate the predictive contribution of search interest more directly, compare forecasting performance across different horizons, and reduce the risk of interpreting stockout-related zero sales as low demand.

The new findings indicate that search features did not reliably improve LightGBM performance and significantly worsened the four-week XGBoost result. Consequently, the conclusion is more cautious: the additional predictive value of search interest has not been established under the current experimental conditions. Stock reconstruction and search-data coverage remain important areas for further improvement.
