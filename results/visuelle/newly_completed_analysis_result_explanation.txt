Newly Completed Analysis: Weekly Sales Forecasting and Search-Interest Contribution
===================================================================================

The newly completed experiments extend the earlier sales analyses by asking whether
Google Trends search interest improves weekly sales forecasts beyond retail history,
calendar information, and planned discounts. The analysis builds calendar-time weekly
sales series for categories, colours, and fabrics; applies a stock-availability filter
based on dated restock records; and evaluates four- and eight-week-ahead forecasts.

Calendar-time split and stock filtering
---------------------------------------
Each weekly target is assigned to a split by its calendar date rather than by season
label. Training target weeks are before 2018-11-26, validation covers 2018-11-26
through 2019-08-05, and the test period begins 2019-08-12. Historical observations
before a forecast origin remain available for lag features across split boundaries;
model fitting uses only training target rows.

Listing-weeks estimated to have no opening stock are excluded from the sales-rate
target. The stock reconstruction is an imperfect proxy: some listing-weeks marked
out of stock still have recorded sales. Thus, the results describe recorded sales
under this filtering procedure, not unconstrained customer demand.

Forecast design and model comparison
------------------------------------
The four- and eight-week experiments forecast sales at the corresponding future
target week using only features available at the forecast origin, including the
origin sales value, calendar and planned-discount information, and, for the search
variants, lagged search interest. The model comparison includes last-value and
trailing-four-week-average baselines, Ridge regression, XGBoost, and LightGBM.
XGBoost and LightGBM each have a no-search model and a search-feature counterpart.
Within each horizon, model variants use the same usable rows, training mask, and
validation/test targets.

On the test set, XGBoost achieved a WAPE of 18.97% at four weeks and 21.68% at
eight weeks without search. LightGBM achieved 19.70% and 21.39%, respectively.
Both boosted-tree models substantially outperformed the last-value baseline in
MAE: XGBoost reduced MAE by about 36% at four weeks and 47% at eight weeks;
LightGBM reduced it by about 35% and 47%. XGBoost had the lowest no-search test
WAPE at four weeks, while LightGBM had the lowest at eight weeks.

Does search improve forecast accuracy?
--------------------------------------
Search impact was measured as test WAPE with search minus test WAPE without
search. Confidence intervals use a paired bootstrap that resamples test weeks,
keeping each week's matched model predictions together. A positive difference
means search worsened WAPE; a negative difference means it improved WAPE.

Model        Horizon  No-search WAPE  Search WAPE  Difference (95% CI), pp
XGBoost      4 weeks       18.97%       20.02%    +1.05 (+0.39, +1.88)
XGBoost      8 weeks       21.68%       22.77%    +1.14 (-0.13, +2.70)
LightGBM     4 weeks       19.70%       19.92%    +0.22 (-0.33, +0.74)
LightGBM     8 weeks       21.39%       20.84%    -0.54 (-1.41, +0.14)

The four-week XGBoost comparison indicates a statistically reliable deterioration
when search is added because its interval is above zero. The other intervals
include zero and do not establish a reliable change. In particular, LightGBM
shows no reliable search improvement at either horizon. Overall, these results
do not demonstrate a consistent search benefit for either boosted-tree model.
This does not prove that search is universally unhelpful; its value may depend on
coverage, the correspondence between search terms and aggregated sales groups,
and the evaluation setting.

Discounts and interpretation
-----------------------------
Discounts are included as planned forecasting features, but this model comparison
does not separately quantify their contribution. The descriptive relationship
between discount bands and sales should not be interpreted causally: discounts
may be introduced later in a product's lifecycle when demand is already changing.
A dedicated feature-ablation experiment would be needed to estimate their
incremental predictive value.

Summary
-------
The newly completed work constructs date-based weekly series, filters estimated
stockout listing-weeks, evaluates four- and eight-week forecasts, compares
baselines and Ridge/XGBoost/LightGBM models with and without search, and assesses
search impact using paired weekly bootstrap confidence intervals. The best
no-search model by test WAPE is XGBoost at four weeks and LightGBM at eight weeks.
Search does not reliably improve LightGBM, and it worsens the four-week XGBoost
result. The main limitations are imperfect stock reconstruction and incomplete
search coverage/alignment.
