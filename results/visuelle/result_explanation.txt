Results Explanation
==================

The analysis investigates whether Google Trends search interest improves weekly
fashion-sales forecasting beyond retail history, calendar variables, and planned
discounts. It extends the earlier proposal and analyses by constructing weekly
sales series in calendar time, filtering listing-weeks estimated to be out of
stock, and evaluating genuine four- and eight-week-ahead forecasts.

Evaluation design
-----------------
Forecast targets are split by calendar date, not by season label. Training target
weeks are before 2018-11-26; validation covers 2018-11-26 through 2019-08-05; and
testing begins 2019-08-12. For each horizon, model variants use the same usable
rows and target weeks. Model fitting uses training rows only. Forecast features
include information available at the origin, including historical sales,
calendar and planned-discount information; search variants also include lagged
search interest.

The stock filter reconstructs opening stock from dated restock records and
excludes listing-weeks estimated to have no opening stock. This reduces the risk
of interpreting stockout-related zero sales as weak demand, but reconstructed
stock is only a proxy: some weeks classified as out of stock still contain
recorded sales. Results therefore measure recorded sales under this filtering
procedure, rather than unconstrained customer demand.

Separate rolling binary-event forecast
--------------------------------------
The retained rolling_forecast_* files evaluate a different forecasting task:
whether a product-retailer listing's cumulative sales over the next four or
eight weeks exceed zero. These are binary-event forecasts, not forecasts of
sales volume or the sales-rate errors reported below. The saved files include
predictions, validation tuning, per-seed metrics, and a metrics summary.

The original experiment script was found in the separately supplied VISUELLE
data folder, not this repository. It retrains every four weeks using training
labels that end before the forecast origin. All 639,740 saved prediction
labels were checked against the supplied weekly sales and matched the
subsequent-sales definition. Origin-week sales and search features require
those observations to be available at forecast time; that assumption and
other feature availability require further verification. The current source
also specifies XGBoost, which is absent from the saved predictions, so the
full saved run has not been reproduced.

The saved test predictions also had severe class imbalance. For four weeks,
40,732 of 43,499 outcomes were positive (93.64%); an always-positive classifier
would achieve 93.64% accuracy. For eight weeks, 20,467 of 20,475 were positive
(99.96%), leaving only eight negatives and an always-positive accuracy of 99.96%.
Accuracy and precision alone would therefore be misleading, and the eight-week
negative-class metrics would be highly unstable. The always-positive baseline
has balanced accuracy of 50% when both classes are present. Interpret
classification metrics alongside this baseline and the class counts, and
verify feature availability before relying on the evaluation.

Decision: retain these files as supplementary historical results, but exclude
them from the project's trend-forecasting performance claims. Selling any
units is not the same as a style rising, and this target does not measure
growth direction, rank emerging styles, predict sales rates, or isolate the
contribution of search. At four weeks, LightGBM's mean balanced accuracy across
five seeds is 78.51%, above the 50% always-positive baseline, but this only
assesses the binary event. At eight weeks its mean balanced accuracy is 56.97%;
the eight negative cases make negative-class performance too fragile to
support a strong conclusion.

Forecast performance
--------------------
The comparison includes last-value and trailing-four-week-average baselines,
Ridge regression, XGBoost, and LightGBM. XGBoost and LightGBM were both evaluated
with and without search features.

On the test set, XGBoost achieved WAPE of 18.97% at four weeks and 21.68% at
eight weeks without search. LightGBM achieved 19.70% and 21.39%. Both boosted
trees substantially reduced MAE relative to the last-value baseline: XGBoost by
approximately 36% at four weeks and 47% at eight weeks; LightGBM by approximately
35% and 47%. XGBoost had the lowest no-search test WAPE at four weeks, and
LightGBM had the lowest at eight weeks.

Contribution of search interest
-------------------------------
Search impact is reported as test WAPE with search minus test WAPE without
search, in percentage points. Confidence intervals come from a paired bootstrap
that resamples test weeks and compares matched predictions.

Model        Horizon  No-search WAPE  Search WAPE  Difference (95% CI), pp
XGBoost      4 weeks       18.97%       20.02%    +1.05 (+0.39, +1.88)
XGBoost      8 weeks       21.68%       22.77%    +1.14 (-0.13, +2.70)
LightGBM     4 weeks       19.70%       19.92%    +0.22 (-0.33, +0.74)
LightGBM     8 weeks       21.39%       20.84%    -0.54 (-1.41, +0.14)

Positive differences indicate higher WAPE with search. The interval for
four-week XGBoost is above zero, indicating a reliable deterioration in this
comparison. The other intervals include zero, so they do not establish a
reliable change. In particular, search does not show a reliable benefit for
LightGBM at either horizon. These results do not prove that search has no value
in general; incomplete temporal coverage, loose alignment between search terms
and aggregated sales groups, and predictive information in retail history may
limit the observed contribution.

Discounts and limitations
-------------------------
Planned discounts are included as model features, but these results do not
isolate their contribution. Observed differences between discount bands are
descriptive, not causal: discounts may be applied later in a product lifecycle
when demand is already changing. A dedicated feature-ablation analysis would be
needed to quantify their incremental predictive value.

The strongest no-search benchmark by test WAPE is XGBoost at four weeks and
LightGBM at eight weeks. Search features do not reliably improve LightGBM and
worsen four-week XGBoost performance. Further work should improve stock
reconstruction and search coverage and alignment before making stronger claims
about unconstrained demand or the general value of search interest.
